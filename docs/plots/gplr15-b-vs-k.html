T-GPLR-15 gate 9 — statistic (b) versus retention K

Measured on a real greedy generation stream: trace probe-tg256.jsonl, model Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, run_id 11634247, 10,200 consecutive-window pairs, W=1, top_k=8, n_expert=256. Self-contained: no external assets, no scripts.

How to read K

K is the per-layer cache's retention depth in token windows — not bytes and not a count of experts. A K-window cache holds min(n_expert, K*W*top_k) expert rows per layer (here W=1, top_k=8, n_expert=256), and one row is that expert's gate+up+down slabs (1,769,472 B). The VRAM axis at the top therefore follows from K rather than being a separate choice. Statistic (b) is defined at a K, so a gate reading is complete only when the K it was read at is named: reading it at K=1–2 (the SDD section 4 reference band, shaded) fails the 0.6 gate, while the curve only crosses 0.6 later, at a VRAM cost the chart prices.
Basis caveat. The 1,769,472 B row size is the model's slot accounting (slot_bytes / n_expert) and it is not uniform: 3 of the 40 layers measured stage 2,039,808 B per row against 1,769,472 B for the other 37. So the true footprint at each K is 1.1% above the axis figures — at K=32 the axis reads 18.12 GB against 18.33 GB from the measured per-layer rows (the run's own full-bank figure is 18,327,011,328 B). The axis is left on the slot basis because that is the basis the SDD section 4 band and the T-GPLR-15 retention records are written in.

Reading this curve

  • The shaded band is the SDD section 4 reference retention: a one-to-two window cache, the only sizing the spec itself declares.
  • The solid blue curve is statistic (b) as measured (10,200 pairs); each marker is a reported K.
  • The thin coloured series are the 4 ladder rungs overlaid — 3-window streams, 80 pairs each. Each is ringed at its structural saturation depth K=2 and flat from there on: past that depth the stream has no older window left, so nothing about the gate can be learned there.
  • The red dashed line is the 0.6 gate. The curve crosses it above the reference band, so the verdict depends on which K an adopting task declares — the reason this was escalated to the owner and closed at the band.
  • The grey dotted line is the T8 oracle ceiling, 0.9385: statistic (b) under unbounded retention, i.e. the whole expert bank resident, which is the outcome the row-set policy exists to avoid. No cache policy of any size can exceed it. The reported K=24 already saturates it.

Why the ladder rungs saturate at K=2

A stream of 3 windows per layer has only 2 predecessor windows, so a cache of depth K ≥ 2 already holds that stream's entire history: there is nothing older left to retain, and (b) must equal the oracle. Capacity cannot bind first either — a K=2 cache holds min(256, 2*W*8) = 16 rows per layer, and any union of 2 8-row windows holds at most that many — so the equality is arithmetic, not a property of the routing process.

That is why the rung series are flat from K=2 on, and why these samples cannot inform the 0.6 threshold: they saturate at a depth set by how much history the trace contains, not by how predictable the routing is. The probe stream carries 256 windows per layer and saturates only at K=24 — the two depths differ by construction, and that difference measures stream length, not routing.

View 2: what this retention is worth in wall-clock terms

Statistic (b) is the fraction of the next window's rows a cache would already hold, so it removes (b) x bytes staged per token from the link. The basis is measured from the trace, not assumed: 14,317,978 B per fetch over 10,240 fetches (8.00 rows each), mean transfer 1,701 us — 8.42 GB/s effective, against the 13.7 GB/s link ceiling. Staging is 78.8% of token time (68.0 ms of 86.4 ms/token over 40 layers), leaving a 18.3 ms/token floor that this lever cannot touch.

Projection, not a claim. It assumes the staged bytes stay on the critical path — they do: staging is 78.8% of token time and the layer graph waits on the copy event — and that removing a share of them removes the same share of the measured per-fetch transfer. The two series differ only in the rate they price that saving at: the blue series uses the 13.7 GB/s link ceiling, the smallest reading of the two, and the one that would also require the transfer path itself to reach the ceiling; the orange series uses the 8.42 GB/s this path is actually measured at, which is what today's mechanism supports. The dotted line is the lever's own asymptote: with every staged byte removed, 18.3 ms/token remains, so no retention choice — and no predictor at all, however accurate — takes TG past 54.5 tok/s on this run. It is one 256-token run on a stream that echoes its own anchor. A PAIRED MEASUREMENT NOW EXISTS at K=1: four arms A,B,A,B in one GPU session gave a reactive mean of 11.953 tok/s and a K=1 mean of 14.021 tok/s (gain +2.068, within-pair spread 0.011). The realized byte reduction was 30.79% against the 30.88% projected here, and the removed bytes converted at 14.29 GB/s — faster than the 13.7 GB/s this view's blue series assumes, which is why the measurement lands near that series rather than the orange one. Marginal removed bytes are cheaper than average bytes because the per-call cost does not shrink with them; that is the proportionality assumption this view rests on, and at 3x extrapolation into F2's regime it is UNTESTED. This view is the decision arithmetic for F2; the K=1 point has since been settled by measurement rather than by projection — it is the ringed marker in view 2, between the two projected series.
